Help

Sounds

Every sound means something. Play them here any time.

For clients

  • Your crossing times come from your O&M instructor. They time you crossing and set the numbers in Settings, or send you a link that sets them.
  • 1. Practise my timing. Tap Begin, then tap again when you think you would have arrived. You hear the difference rather than being told a number.
  • 2. Time it from a signal. Press Ready and wait. When the signal comes, press again when your crossing time is up.
  • 3. Compare practice. The app plays a sample warning time. Decide whether it was longer or shorter than the time you need, then feel the difference.
  • 4. At the street. The same judgement with real traffic.

Never count. The point is to feel the length of time, not to measure it.

For instructors

  • Setting up. Time at least three crossings and use the longest. Enter the first-half and full-street times in Settings, or send them to the client as a link from Settings → Share time settings. The link can carry your email and a short client code, never a name.
  • Margin of error. You choose and set it. The app defaults to 0.40 s; 0.50 s has historically been taught with a stopwatch. There is no research yet on the right value.
  • Directions. A vehicle approaching from the left is compared with the first-half time; from the right, with the full-street time.
  • Feedback modes. Audio, visual, or both, under Accessibility. Visual-only supports Deaf clients; vibration supports DeafBlind clients in the installed app.
  • Progress. The client can show you the Progress screen, or email or copy a written report. Data never leaves their device unless they send it.
  • Experimental. The adaptive margin and the background-noise check are off by default. Both are unproven; turn them on only to try them.

What the sounds mean

Your own taps

This plays back where you tapped.

Close enough

A short, bright chime. You were within the margin.

Outside the margin

A longer, low, buzzing pulse. You were too far off.

A sample warning time

In Compare practice: one sound that grows louder, like a vehicle approaching, then stops. Its length is the warning time.

The start signal

In Time it from a signal: two quick blips mean the clock has started.

The important part

What matters is the distance between your sound and the reference sound. The further apart they are, the further off you were. Never count seconds — listen for the distance between them.
